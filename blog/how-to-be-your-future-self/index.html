How to Be Your Future Self: A Daily Practice for Women

By Perla, founder of Aethernal · October 28, 2026 · 7 min read

It's Sunday night. You're in bed with your phone, typing a note that starts with "This year, I will..." and you can almost see her: calmer, braver, living in a life that finally fits.

Then Monday comes. The alarm, the snooze, the same coffee, the same inbox, and by Wednesday that woman feels like someone you made up.

If you've been wondering how to be your future self instead of just dreaming about her, this is for you. Below is a simple daily practice in three steps: see her, feel her, act as her. The third step is the one most people miss, and it's the one that turns a vague vision into real choices you make this week.

What does future self mean, really?

Your future self is simply you, later: the woman who will wake up in your body a month, a year, or ten years from now, carrying the results of what you choose today.

That's the plain future self meaning. The deeper one is this: she isn't a fantasy version of you, she's a continuation of you. She has your laugh, your stubborn streak, your soft spots. She just has a few more chapters behind her.

For example, picture the woman you'll be next December. She isn't a stranger with a perfect morning routine. She's you, who kept one small promise for long enough that it became who she is.

Why does your future self feel like a stranger?

Your future self feels distant because, in a real sense, your mind treats her a bit like someone else.

One brain imaging study found that when people thought about themselves ten years in the future, their brain activity looked less like thinking about their current self, and people with a bigger gap tended to make more impatient choices with money. A separate study found that people who saw digitally aged images of their own faces chose to set aside more money in a hypothetical retirement account.

These are small, specific studies, not a promise about your life. But they point to something you've probably felt: it's hard to take care of someone you can't picture. When "future me" is a blur, the couch, the scroll, and the extra glass of wine win every time.

So the work isn't to try harder. It's to make her clearer, closer, and more familiar.

How to be your future self: the daily practice

The practice takes about ten minutes a day, and it has three steps you repeat: see her, feel her, act as her.

You can do it with your morning coffee or right before bed. All you need is a notebook and a few quiet minutes. Here's how each step works.

Step 1: See her (future self visualization)

Close your eyes and picture one ordinary moment in your future self's day, not the big highlight reel.

Skip the wedding, the book launch, the beach house. Instead, see her on a normal Tuesday. Where does she wake up? What does she reach for first? How does she speak to herself in the mirror?

This kind of future self visualization works best when it's small and specific. "I'm successful" is fog. "I'm walking to my studio at 8 a.m. with my coat open because I'm not rushing" is something your mind can actually hold. If you want a gentle way into this, try [visualizing your dream life starting with one ordinary Tuesday](/blog/how-to-visualize-your-dream-life/), or use a [10-minute visualization meditation](/blog/visualization-meditation-for-manifestation/) to settle in first.

Step 2: Feel her (future self journaling)

Now open your notebook and write a few lines as her, in the present tense, about how she feels.

Not what she owns. How she feels. Future self journaling is where the picture becomes a feeling you can recognize in your own body.

Try a short entry like: "I feel steady in the mornings now. I don't check my phone until I've had my tea. I trust myself with my own time." Then add one line she'd say to you today: "You don't have to fix everything this week. Just keep the morning."

That line from her to you is the heart of the practice. It turns her from an idea into someone on your side.

Step 3: Act as her (the step most people miss)

Here's the one I promised: choose one small action your future self would take today, and decide exactly when and where you'll do it.

Most people stop at seeing and feeling. It's lovely, and it fades by lunch. Acting is where you be your future self now, in a tiny, real way, instead of waiting until you've "become" her.

Make it almost too small. If she moves her body every day, you walk for ten minutes. If she's a writer, you write one paragraph. If she has calm finances, you check your account balance without flinching.

Then attach it to a moment with an if-then plan: "When I pour my morning coffee, I'll write one paragraph before opening email." A large review of research on these if-then plans found they were linked to better follow-through on goals. The plan matters as much as the wish.

How do you turn a vague vision into choices this week?

Turn your vision into a week by picking one quality of your future self and giving it one small action per day.

A vague vision sounds like "I want to be healthier and more confident." A weekly version picks one quality, say "she keeps promises to herself," and makes it concrete.

Here's what that week might look like:

  • Monday: Write down one promise for the week, small enough to keep.
  • Tuesday: Do it, then write one line about how it felt.
  • Wednesday: Notice the moment you almost skipped it. Write what got in the way.
  • Thursday: Adjust the time or place so it's easier.
  • Friday: Do it again, even if it's messy.
  • Weekend: Read back your notes and ask her, "What do you want me to keep?"

This is also why a pretty board on the wall often doesn't change much on its own. If yours has started to feel like wallpaper, here's [why your vision board is not working and how to fix it](/blog/why-your-vision-board-is-not-working/). A vision only moves when it lands on a calendar.

What if you feel stuck or scared of your future?

If you feel stuck, start smaller than feels reasonable, and let your future self be kind to you rather than disappointed in you.

A lot of people ask some version of "I'm 25, I feel stuck, and I'm scared of my own future. Is that just me?" It isn't. Fear often shows up exactly when you start caring about who you're becoming.

So when you journal as her, notice her tone. Would the woman you're becoming really sneer at you for a hard month? Or would she say, "I remember that season. You were doing better than you thought."

For example, if all you can manage today is drinking a glass of water and going to bed on time, that counts. So many people who go deep into self-improvement end up saying the same thing: it comes back to the basics, done gently, again and again.

Quick answers

How do I become my future self fast?

There's no shortcut, but you can start today instead of someday. Picture one ordinary moment in her life, write a few lines as her, and do one tiny thing she'd do before tonight. Small actions repeated daily are how you become your future self.

What is future self journaling?

Future self journaling means writing in the present tense as the woman you're becoming. You describe how she feels, what she does, and what she'd say to you now. It helps turn a fuzzy vision into something familiar and personal.

Is "be your future self now" just fake it till you make it?

Not quite. You aren't pretending to have a life you don't have yet. You're borrowing one small choice from her and making it yours today, which is real, not fake.

How long does future self visualization take?

A few minutes is enough. Two or three minutes of seeing one specific scene, followed by a short journal entry and one planned action, makes a complete ten-minute practice.
